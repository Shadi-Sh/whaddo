# Whaddo? — Problem Statement

**Status:** Approved wording supplied by Shadi on 26 October 2026. The claims below are **unvalidated assumptions**, not established research findings. Approval of the wording does not validate the problem.

## Statement

Students and early-career professionals may struggle to decide how to spend their free time in a satisfying way. Whether they have a 15-minute break between lectures or an entire afternoon available, they may feel overwhelmed by too many options or struggle to think of any suitable activities.
Their choices may also be limited by contextual factors such as available time, location, energy level, available resources, or whether they are alone or in a group.
Additionally, people may forget activities they previously wanted to try or find it difficult to discover new ones that suit their interests and circumstances.
As a result, they may repeatedly fall back on familiar routines, spend more time deciding than actually doing something, or feel dissatisfied with how they spend their free time.

## What needs evidence

- Whether students and early-career professionals are the right initial audience.
- Whether difficulty choosing or finding activities occurs, how often, and with what consequences.
- Which contextual factors matter in real situations, including short breaks and longer free periods.
- Whether forgetting intended activities or discovering new ones is a meaningful unmet need.
- Whether familiar routines are unwanted, decision time is excessive, or satisfaction is reduced.

No user-research evidence is documented in this repository yet. These assumptions do not establish demand for an app or imply approved features.
